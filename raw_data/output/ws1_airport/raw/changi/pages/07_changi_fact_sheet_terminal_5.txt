Fact Sheet: Changi Airport Terminal 5
15 Apr 2026
Changi Airport Terminal 5 (T5) is scheduled to open in the mid-2030s, providing additional annual passenger capacity of 50 million to meet the rising demand for air travel.
T5’s design principles include a focus on modularity and resilience, with sustainability at its core as a Green Mark Platinum Super Low Energy building powered by one of Singapore’s largest rooftop solar systems.
T5 will leverage automation, robotics and intelligent systems at scale, including the adoption of AI and data‑driven integrated operations to enhance efficiency, manpower productivity and passenger experience.
To ensure T5 is well-connected to the rest of the island,  a ground transportation centre located within the terminal will provide travellers with easy access to train, bus, taxi and other transport services across Singapore.
An automated people mover system will link T5 to T2, facilitating seamless transfers to the other terminals.
Background
First announced in 2013, Changi Airport’s T5 is a project undertaken by the Ministry of Transport (MOT), the Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore (CAAS) and Changi Airport Group (CAG).
During the Covid-19 pandemic, the T5 project was paused for two years. During this time, MOT, CAAS and CAG re-assessed the trajectory of aviation growth, and reviewed T5’s design to make it more modular, and to enhance its resilience and sustainability.
Post-Covid, passenger traffic at Changi Airport has strongly recovered and reached new highs. Changi Airport registered a record annual traffic of 69.98 million passenger movements for the year 2025, and now links Singapore directly to about 170 cities globally, with close to 100 airlines operating more than 7,300 flights weekly. More than two million tonnes of airfreight throughput was recorded for 2025.
Changi Airport is the fourth busiest international air hub in the world, and one of the most connected gateways to the region. Two of the top 10 busiest international global flight routes in 2025 originate from or end in Singapore, while five of the top 10 busiest international routes in Southeast Asia take off or land in Singapore. Changi Airport is also among the 10 busiest international air cargo hubs.
Airports Council International and International Air Transport Association have projected that the Asia-Pacific would be one of the fastest-growing regions for air travel over the next two decades.
To enable Singapore to ride on the long-term growth of aviation, the additional capacity from T5 will strengthen Changi Airport’s position as a leading air hub for the region and beyond, and will ensure that future generations of Singaporeans can continue to benefit from Changi’s global connectivity.
T5 will be located within the 1,080-hectare Changi East development that is almost as big as the land area of today’s Changi Airport. It is designed with the flexibility to be built in phases, in tandem with travel demand growth. When the first phase opens in the mid-2030s, T5 will be able to handle about 50 million passenger movements a year.
Highlights of Terminal 5
Enhances multi-model connectivity across air, land and sea
T5 offers the possibility of integrating air, land and sea connectivity, enhancing Changi Airport’s role as a global hub by enabling seamless passenger travel, efficient cargo transfers, and stronger links to regional markets and beyond.
T5 will be well-connected to the rest of Singapore. Through the ground transportation centre located within the terminal, travellers can access train, bus, taxi and other transport services to connect to the rest of Singapore.
Thomson-East Coast Line will be extended to Terminal 5 to connect it directly to the city centre, and further northwards to the Johor Bahru-Singapore Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link terminal at Woodlands North. The Cross Island Line is also planned to connect Terminal 5 to other major hubs such as Punggol Digital District and Jurong Lake District.
Aviation Park Station on the Cross Island Line is planned to serve the wider airport community at the Changi Airfreight Centre and Changi East Industrial Zone.
New roads along the Changi Southern Corridor will connect Terminal 5 to Tanah Merah Coast Road, East Coast Parkway and Pan Island Expressway.
On the sea connectivity front, T5’s proximity to Tanah Merah Ferry Terminal allows the possibility of facilitating seamless air-sea transfers to neighbouring destinations.
A green and sustainable terminal building
T5 will be a Green Mark Platinum Super Low Energy Building and have one of the largest rooftop solar systems in Singapore, with the potential to generate enough solar energy to power about 20,000 4-room HDB flats for a year.
It will have a Distributed District Cooling System to improve energy efficiency and lower carbon emissions, as well as innovative and efficient system designs, including building and energy management systems.
T5 will also be ready for viable alternative fuels, including the use of Sustainable Aviation Fuel, and for the provision of fixed ground power and cooling for aircraft parked at the gates.
An airport that is operationally and climate resilient
Drawing on lessons learnt from the Covid-19 pandemic, T5 will be designed with the flexibility to operate as smaller sub-terminals when needed, with space that could be converted for the handling of contingencies, such as health testing operations or the segregation of high-risk passengers.
Specialised provisions to reduce the transmission of diseases will also be deployed in T5. This includes contactless systems at passenger touchpoints, as well as enhanced ventilation systems that can be activated during a pandemic.
Beyond T5, Changi East is designed to withstand extreme weather and climate change effects. The airfield is designed with an elevation of 5.5m above sea level. Aircraft taxiways are sloped to allow rainwater to drain off.
The airfield drainage system is engineered to manage stormwater effectively during high tide especially with rising sea levels, and handle intense rainfall from extreme storms.
Designed for the deployment of automation at scale
T5 will leverage technology to automate and digitalise airport operations, to enhance efficiency and passenger experience, as well as increase manpower productivity. Changi Airport will embark on an airport-wide transformation programme focussing on next-generation integrated operations for total airport management through the use of intelligent systems and data.
A key enabler of next-generation integrated operations is the Integrated Operations Centre, where operational stakeholders have common situational awareness at the hub level, aided by collaboration tools to make data-driven decisions.
In addition to various ongoing trials to enhance airport operations via automation, video analytics, AI and robotics are also being trialled for wider implementation in Changi Airport, including T5. For example, video analytics and AI are used to actively track aircraft turnaround activities and predict potential delays in flight departures. With full visibility of the situation, ground handlers can better deploy resources to mitigate potential delays.
In another trial, baggage robots will be tested for operations in the terminals and under rain and lightning conditions at the aircraft stands, to enable continuous operations and timely delivery of baggage for passengers.
T5 will be designed to accommodate and facilitate deployment of technology at scale. These efforts will provide local enterprises with an opportunity to build capabilities in new, emerging areas such as robotics and artificial intelligence, and ultimately create quality jobs for Singaporeans within the airport ecosystem.
A place connecting all Singaporeans
T5 will be a co-creation of the Changi aviation community, the government and the public. It will reinforce Changi Airport as a national icon and ensure that it will continue to be a place of shared experiences across generations of Singaporeans, where we welcome and send off loved ones, and forge precious memories with friends and family.
Located at the doorstep of T5, the Changi East Urban District (CEUD) will serve as a vibrant business and lifestyle destination, providing new spaces for Singaporeans to connect, work and play. The Government is leading the concept planning of the CEUD.
Progress on Terminal 5 Construction
In 2025, several major contracts were awarded, marking key milestones in the development of Terminal 5. CAG announced the award of T5 substructure works for the construction of foundations and basements for the main passenger terminal and ground transportation centre, as well as key underground systems linking T5 with existing airport infrastructure. Contracts were also awarded for the construction of intra-terminal tunnels, which will serve as underground connections within T5 for passenger transfer and baggage handling systems and essential support utilities.
Major tunnelling works connecting T5 and T2 have been completed, comprising three bored tunnels approximately 1.7 km in length each. Two of these tunnels are dedicated to automated people movers for passenger transfer, and one for baggage transfer. Works are in progress to connect the completed tunnel stretches at both ends to the respective terminal buildings.
Timeline
2013:
Prime Minister Lee Hsien Loong spoke about the plans for T5 and Changi East in his National Day Rally speech. Shortly after, the Changi 2036 Steering Committee, which was established to develop a holistic long-term plan for the expansion of Changi Airport, announced its recommendations to construct T5 and implement a three-runway system at Changi.
2014:
Land preparation works for Changi East and planning for T5 began in 2014, including functional design studies and concept development for the terminal.
2018:
CAG announced the appointment of the Master Building Consultants (MBC) in 2018, to provide architectural design and engineering consultancy services for the development of T5. The Master Civil Consultants (MCC) were also appointed, to provide consultancy services for the design of infrastructure at the landside and airside areas outside of the T5 buildings.
2020:
A two-year pause of the T5 project was announced in 2020, due to the Covid-19 pandemic. The aim was to assess the impact of Covid-19 on aviation growth, learn from the pandemic experience, and review T5’s design to meet the needs of post-pandemic travel.
2022:
The resumption of work on the T5 project was announced in 2022. MOT, CAAS and CAG had taken the opportunity of the two-year pause to comprehensively review the T5 design to make it more modular, resilient and sustainable. Construction of T5 will commence in about two years for T5 to meet the anticipated demand for air travel around the mid-2030s.
2025:
Prime Minister Lawrence Wong breaks ground for Terminal 5, marking the start of construction. Works have since progressed steadily, with major contracts awarded for construction of substructure works, intra-terminal tunnels, and supporting airside infrastructure.
2026:
As part of public engagement efforts, CAG launched the “T5 In the Making” exhibition in January 2026, offering visitors a closer look at Terminal 5’s vision, design and future innovations.
Changi Airport T5 Key Details
When is Changi Airport T5 scheduled to open?
The first phase of Terminal 5 is scheduled to open in the mid-2030s to handle the increase in travel demand.
What are the key features of Changi Airport T5?
It will be a mega-yet-cosy terminal, featuring a modular, pandemic-resilient design, advanced automation like baggage robots, and the vibrant Changi East Urban District. T5’s design concept is characterised by a unique collection of overlapping curved roofs with varying heights. The ‘roof leaves’ break the mega terminal building into smaller spaces that are more human scale. Combined with natural light and landscaping at appropriate spaces, the terminal will have a cosy yet uplifting feel.
Which MRT lines will connect to Changi Airport T5?
The Thomson-East Coast Line and the Cross Island Line will both be extended to serve the terminal directly.
How will T5 be connected to the rest of Singapore?
It connects via MRT, buses, taxis, new Southern Corridor roads, and air-sea transfers at Tanah Merah Ferry Terminal.
What sustainability features will T5 have?
It will be a Green Mark Platinum building featuring a massive rooftop solar system and sustainable aviation fuel readiness.
